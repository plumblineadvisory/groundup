What Marketing Actually Is

Weekly Objective Understand what marketing really is — not ads, not tricks, but the work of communicating your value clearly to the right people at the right time. Learn how The Paddock markets itself and begin identifying who your own customer actually is.
🗂 Ground Up Plan — Session 3

This Month You're Building: Your Marketing Plan

This is Ground Up Plan Deliverable #3. Combined with your Personal Brand Statement and Business Budget, you'll be three-quarters of the way to a complete business plan.

Key Concepts

Marketing is not advertising — it's communication Most people think marketing means running ads. It doesn't. Marketing is every way your business communicates its value to the world — the name on the door, the way your staff greets guests, the smell when someone walks in, your Instagram bio, a customer's word-of-mouth recommendation. The Paddock's most powerful marketing isn't a paid ad. It's the story of what it is: a café where redemption is served daily, staffed by people rebuilding their lives. That story — told authentically — is more compelling than any billboard.
The four Ps — still the foundation of every marketing plan Product (what you're selling), Price (what you charge), Place (where and how customers buy it), and Promotion (how you tell people about it). These four decisions together define your marketing strategy. Most entrepreneurs default to thinking only about promotion — running ads, posting on social media. But a business with the wrong product, at the wrong price, in the wrong place, can't be saved by great promotion. Get the first three right before you worry about the fourth.
Know exactly who your customer is — before you try to reach them The biggest marketing mistake new businesses make is trying to market to everyone. "Everyone could use my product" is not a marketing strategy. It's a way to spend money reaching nobody effectively. The Paddock's core customer in Shelbyville is not everyone in town — it's the person who values community, wants a warm place to start their morning, and cares about supporting local businesses with a mission. Knowing that shapes every marketing decision they make.
Your story is your most powerful marketing asset In 2026, consumers are drowning in advertising. What cuts through isn't polish — it's authenticity. Sprout Social's 2026 Content Strategy Report found that consumers ranked human-generated, authentic content as their number one priority over AI-generated content. For someone rebuilding their life and building a business from the ground up, authenticity is not something you have to manufacture. It's the most powerful thing you have. Your story — told honestly — is marketing that can't be faked or bought.
Marketing builds trust — and trust is what actually drives sales Nobody buys from a business they don't trust. Marketing's real job is trust-building — consistently showing up, delivering on promises, being honest about what you are and aren't. In a small town like Shelbyville, trust is built face-to-face and word-of-mouth before it's built on social media. The best marketing strategy for a new local business is almost always: be excellent, treat people well, and make it easy for happy customers to tell someone else.

AI & Marketing Strategy — Week 1

🌱 AI Basics

AI is a genuinely useful marketing tool — it can help you brainstorm names, write captions, research competitors, and draft your brand story. But it works best when you give it specific information about your business, your customer, and your values. Vague prompts produce generic output. Specific prompts produce useful output.

Define Your Customer with AI In Claude.ai, type: "Help me define my ideal customer. My business is [your idea]. It's based in Shelbyville, Kentucky. Ask me five questions that will help me get really specific about who my customer is — their age, lifestyle, values, where they spend time, and what problem I'm solving for them." — Answer the questions. Save the result. This is the foundation of your Marketing Plan.
🚀 Going Deeper

A customer persona is a detailed description of your ideal customer — not a demographic summary, but a portrait of a real person. Marketers use personas to make every content and advertising decision: "Would Sarah (our persona) care about this post?" AI can help you build one that's genuinely useful rather than generic.

Build Your Customer Persona In Claude.ai, type: "Based on my business idea [description] and target customer answers [paste your answers from the AI Basics exercise], help me build a one-paragraph customer persona. Give this person a name, an age, a daily routine, what they value, what frustrates them, and why they'd be drawn to my business specifically." — Save this in your Google Drive Marketing Plan document. It anchors every decision you make this session.

Your Brand & Your Story

Weekly Objective Understand what a brand actually is — beyond a logo — and build the foundational elements of your own brand. Name your business, write your tagline, define your voice, and craft the origin story that will make your customer choose you over anyone else.
☕

Key Concepts

A brand is not a logo — it's a feeling A logo is a symbol. A brand is the sum of every experience someone has with your business — what they see, feel, hear, and remember. When someone thinks of The Paddock, they think: warm, mission-driven, community, hope, good coffee. That's the brand. The logo is just the label on the outside of it. Building a brand means deciding intentionally what feeling you want people to walk away with — and then engineering every touchpoint of your business to create that feeling.
Your name is the first thing people will judge — make it count A good business name is memorable, easy to spell, relevant to what you do or who you are, and available as a social media handle. It doesn't have to be clever, but it shouldn't be confusing. Some of the strongest small business names are simply the owner's name plus what they do. Some are a single evocative word. The test: can someone hear it once and remember it tomorrow? Can they spell it to search for you online? Would they be comfortable saying it to a friend?
Brand voice: how you sound is part of the brand Brand voice is the personality that comes through in everything you write — social media captions, a menu description, a thank-you note, a text reply to a customer. Some brands are warm and conversational. Some are bold and direct. Some are funny. Some are professional. What's not an option: boring and generic. Your brand voice should sound like the person who built this business — which means it should sound like you. Authenticity, again, is the asset.
Your origin story is your most powerful content Why did you start this business? What brought you here? For someone building something after a period of difficulty, the origin story is often the most compelling thing about the brand. You don't have to share everything. But sharing something real — "I started this because..." — creates an emotional connection that no amount of advertising budget can replicate. People buy from people, not from businesses. Your origin story is the proof that a person is behind this.
Consistency builds recognition — recognition builds trust The most powerful thing a small business can do with its brand is be consistent. Same name, same colors, same voice, same quality — every time, everywhere. Consumers in 2026 interact with hundreds of brands daily. The ones that break through are the ones that look and sound the same whether you see them on Instagram, their website, or a business card. Consistency is not boring — it's the thing that makes a brand feel real.

AI & Brand Building — Week 2

🌱 AI Basics

Naming a business is one of the hardest creative tasks — and one where AI is genuinely useful. You can describe your business idea, your values, and your customer, and ask AI to generate dozens of name options in seconds. You won't use any of them as-is, but they'll spark ideas and combinations you wouldn't have thought of alone.

Generate Business Names In Claude.ai, type: "Help me name my business. It's [your idea], based in Shelbyville, Kentucky. My customer is [describe them briefly]. My brand should feel [warm/bold/simple/etc.]. Give me 15 name ideas — some descriptive, some evocative, some based on my story. Include a one-sentence explanation of each." — Circle your top three. You'll pick one this week.
🚀 Going Deeper

AI can help you write your brand story — but only if you give it the raw material. The best approach: write your origin story in your own rough words first (messy, honest, unpolished), then use AI to help you shape it into something that's still yours but communicates more clearly. The AI shouldn't write your story. It should help you tell it better.

Draft Your Brand Story Write 5–8 sentences about why you're starting this business — include what brought you here, what you've been through, and what you want to build. Then paste it into Claude.ai with: "Help me shape this into a 3–4 sentence brand story that's honest, warm, and would make someone in Shelbyville want to support this business. Keep my voice — just make it cleaner." — Save both versions in your Marketing Plan document.

Online Presence & Content That Works

Weekly Objective Learn how to build a social media presence that actually earns attention — without a production budget or marketing team. Understand the content types that work in 2026, choose the right platforms for your customer, and create your first week of real content for your business.
☕

Key Concepts

Choose one or two platforms — and show up consistently The most common small business social media mistake is being on every platform, posting inconsistently on all of them. A business that posts great content three times a week on Instagram will out-perform a business on six platforms that posts sporadically on each. For a local business in Shelbyville, Facebook is still where the community lives — it's where events are shared, local groups exist, and word travels. Instagram is where you show what you make. Start with those two before adding anything else.
The content mix that works for small businesses in 2026 The most effective small business social media content follows a simple mix: about 40% educational or helpful content (tips, how-tos, behind-the-scenes), 30% storytelling (your origin, your process, your people), 20% promotional (actual product or service posts), and 10% community (sharing others' content, responding to customers, local events). The businesses that only post promotional content — buy this, sale on this, try this — burn out their audiences fast. Lead with value first.
Short video is the highest-reach format of 2026 Instagram Reels generate 22% more interactions than regular video posts. TikTok drives an average engagement rate of 3.7% — the highest of any major platform. And you don't need a camera crew, lighting equipment, or editing software. A smartphone, natural light, and something worth watching is enough. For a food or café business, "how it's made" content is some of the most naturally engaging content on the internet. Show the process. Show the hands. Show the real.
Google Business Profile — the most overlooked free marketing tool Before a customer walks into any local business in 2026, they Google it. A Google Business Profile is free, takes 20 minutes to set up, and directly affects whether you show up in local search results. It shows your hours, photos, contact information, and customer reviews. For a new local business, setting up a Google Business Profile is more valuable in its first week than any paid advertising campaign. It is non-negotiable for a local business in Shelbyville.
Respond to everything — that's your marketing A Sprout Social 2025 survey found that 73% of consumers said if a brand doesn't respond on social media, they'll buy from a competitor. In the early days of your business, every comment, every DM, every review is a marketing opportunity. Respond to all of them — warmly, quickly, in your brand voice. A business that responds to a negative Google review professionally and kindly wins more new customers than the ones who never review at all. Your responsiveness is visible. It signals that you care.

AI & Content Creation — Week 3

🌱 AI Basics

Writing social media captions is one of the most practical everyday uses of AI. You describe the post — what it's showing, who it's for, what you want people to feel — and AI drafts the caption. Then you edit it to sound like you. This alone can cut the time it takes to post from 20 minutes to 5.

Write Your First Three Captions In Claude.ai, type: "Write three Instagram captions for my business [name and description]. Post 1: a photo of my product. Post 2: a behind-the-scenes shot of me making it. Post 3: a personal story about why I started this business. Each caption should be 2–3 sentences, end with a question or call-to-action, and sound warm and local — not corporate."
🚀 Going Deeper

A content calendar is a simple plan — what you'll post, on which platform, on which day. Having one means you're never staring at a blank screen wondering what to post. AI can help you build a 30-day content calendar in minutes — mixing the educational, story, promotional, and community content types that work best for small businesses.

Build Your 30-Day Content Calendar In Claude.ai, type: "Build me a 30-day social media content calendar for [your business name], posting 3 times per week on Facebook and Instagram. Use this content mix: 40% helpful/educational, 30% story-driven, 20% promotional, 10% community. Give me the day, platform, content type, and a one-sentence description of what each post would show." — Save this in your Marketing Plan document as Section 3.

Session 3 · Week 4

Upselling, Retention & Your First Ten Customers

Weekly Objective Understand why keeping customers is more valuable than finding new ones — and learn how to upsell naturally, build loyalty, and turn your first ten customers into your most powerful marketing asset. Then pull everything from this session together for your Marketing Plan capstone.
☕

Key Concepts

Retention is your highest-return marketing investment Acquiring a new customer costs five to seven times more than keeping an existing one. A 5% increase in retention can boost profits by up to 95%. Your best customers — the ones who return every week, recommend you to friends, and forgive the occasional mistake — are worth far more than a dozen one-time buyers. In a small town like Shelbyville, a loyal regular customer is also a walking advertisement. Treat them accordingly.
Upselling is a service — not a sales tactic Done well, an upsell is a recommendation that genuinely improves the customer's experience. "Would you like a pastry with that — we just pulled them out of the oven" is an upsell. It's also helpful information. Done badly, an upsell feels like pressure. The difference is whether the person making the offer genuinely thinks the customer would enjoy it. At The Paddock, every server who knows the menu well can upsell naturally — because recommending something you actually believe in doesn't feel like selling at all.
Your first ten customers are your most important marketing decision The first ten customers of any small business have disproportionate influence on everything that follows. If they love the experience, they tell people. If they feel underwhelmed, they're silent or worse. This means your first ten customers deserve extraordinary attention — not because they paid more, but because the word-of-mouth they generate is worth more than any ad campaign. Find them deliberately. Know who they are. Follow up. Ask what they thought. Make them feel like co-founders of something worth supporting.
Word of mouth is still the most trusted form of marketing In 2026, with AI-generated content everywhere and ad fatigue at an all-time high, word of mouth has never been more valuable. A recommendation from a trusted friend outweighs any ad. In Shelbyville, where everyone knows someone who knows someone, word of mouth isn't just marketing — it's infrastructure. The fastest way to grow a local small business is to make your current customers so happy they can't help talking about you. That's a strategy, not a hope.
Ask for the review — every time Happy customers rarely leave reviews without being asked. Unhappy customers almost always do. This creates a natural imbalance that hurts small businesses who don't actively request reviews. The fix is simple: after a positive interaction, ask. "If you enjoyed it, we'd love a Google review — it helps us a lot." A business with 30 genuine five-star reviews in Shelbyville is more trusted than one with no reviews and a beautiful Instagram. Reviews are marketing you earn through service.

AI & Customer Retention — Week 4

🌱 AI Basics

AI can help you write the follow-up messages that turn a first-time buyer into a returning customer. A thank-you message after a first purchase, a check-in after a week, a "we miss you" note after a month — these touchpoints build loyalty, and AI can help you draft them in minutes. The key: make them feel personal, not automated.

Write Your Customer Follow-Up Sequence In Claude.ai, type: "Write three short customer follow-up messages for my business [name and description]: (1) a thank-you after a first purchase, (2) a check-in one week later, (3) a 'we miss you' message after a month of no contact. Each should be 2–3 sentences, warm, and in my brand voice: [your three brand voice words from Week 2]."
🚀 Going Deeper

A loyalty strategy doesn't require a fancy app or a punch card program. It requires a clear answer to: what will you do to make customers want to come back? AI can help you design a simple loyalty program — including how to structure it, what rewards feel valuable to your customer, and how to talk about it in your marketing.

Design Your Loyalty Strategy In Claude.ai, type: "Help me design a simple customer loyalty strategy for my business [description]. My customer is [your persona]. I don't have a big budget. Suggest three practical ways to reward repeat customers, one way to encourage referrals, and one way to re-engage customers who haven't bought in a while. Keep it simple enough that I can actually execute it alone." — Add this to your Marketing Plan as Section 5.

What Makes a Marketing Plan Credible

A credible marketing plan is specific. Not "I'll use social media" — but "I'll post three times a week on Facebook and Instagram, using this content mix, targeting this person, with this voice." Not "I'll get customers by word of mouth" — but "here are my first ten customers by name or type, here's how I'll reach them, and here's what I'll ask them to do."

Vague marketing plans are wishes. Specific ones are strategies. Your capstone should have no vague sentences in it.
