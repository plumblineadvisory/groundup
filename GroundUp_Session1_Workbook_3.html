First Impressions & Guest Experience

This Week's Focus The first seven seconds of any guest interaction matters more than almost anything else. This week you'll learn why — and start thinking about what that means for your business idea.
☕

What You Need to Know

First impressions happen in 7 secondsBefore a guest even reaches the counter at The Paddock, they've already formed an impression. Eye contact, posture, energy in the room — it all counts before a word is spoken.
Great service has five partsAcknowledge every guest immediately. Be warm — genuinely, not scripted. Know what you're doing. Respect their time. And when something goes wrong, how you handle it is what they remember.
Service is a skill — anyone can learn itNobody is born knowing how to serve people well. The best servers at The Paddock weren't the best on their first day. What gets people good fast is paying attention, asking questions, and taking feedback seriously.
Your experience as a customer is your best teacherThink about the last time someone made you feel genuinely welcomed. What did they do? That's a lesson. The last time you felt ignored? That's a lesson too. You've been taking notes your whole life — you just didn't know it.
70%
of customers leave because of how they were treated — not because of price or product.
7 sec
is all it takes to form a first impression — of a person, a place, or a business.

Session 1 · Week 2

Communication Skills That Open Doors

This Week's Focus Talking and communicating are not the same thing. This week you'll learn the difference — and why being a great listener is one of the most valuable skills you can have at work and in business.
☕

What You Need to Know

Talking and communicating are differentTalking is making sounds. Communicating is making sure the other person actually understood what you meant — and felt heard themselves. Most misunderstandings at work happen not because people disagree, but because they weren't actually communicating.
Listening is the most underrated skill at workThe guests who feel most valued at The Paddock are almost always the ones who feel most heard. Active listening means: eye contact, no interrupting, and asking one follow-up question. It takes practice. It changes how people experience you.
How you say it matters as much as what you sayTone, speed, and body language carry as much information as your words — sometimes more. "I'll check on that" said warmly is reassurance. Said flatly while walking away, it's dismissal. You are communicating even when you're not speaking.
Your story is your most powerful toolPeople remember stories. They forget bullet points. If you can tell the story of who you are and what you're building — honestly and clearly — you will be more memorable than almost anyone else in any room. That story is the foundation of your Personal Brand Statement.
93%
of communication is non-verbal — tone, body language, and energy carry more than words alone.
2x
more likely to be hired — people who communicate their story clearly stand out above stronger résumés.

Session 1 · Week 3

Handling Hard Conversations with Grace

This Week's Focus Most unhappy customers don't complain — they just leave and tell their friends. The ones who do complain are actually giving you a gift. This week you'll learn how to handle it well.
☕

What You Need to Know

A complaint is a gift — if you treat it that wayCustomers who complain and get helped well become more loyal than customers who never had a problem at all. The complaint gives you a chance to fix it and keep them. Most businesses don't get that chance.
The LAST framework — four steps that always workListen — let them finish without interrupting. Apologize — sincerely, not defensively. Solve — fix the problem, even if it wasn't your fault. Thank — them for bringing it to your attention. In that order. Every time.
Your emotional state is contagiousIf you come to work anxious or irritable, guests and teammates feel it. If you're calm and present, that travels too. Staying grounded under pressure is something you're already working on in recovery — it turns out those same skills make you an exceptional hospitality professional.
Scripts are a starting point — not a scriptScripts give you language when your brain goes blank under pressure. But the best service professionals internalize the purpose — make the guest feel valued — and then speak naturally. A script said robotically is worse than no script. The goal is to sound like yourself while hitting all the right notes.
95%
of customers will give a business a second chance if their complaint was handled well and quickly.
1 in 26
unhappy customers actually complains. The other 25 just leave — and tell their friends.

Session 1 · Week 4

Showing Up — Reliability & Professionalism

This Week's Focus The most professionally valuable thing you can be is someone people don't have to worry about. This week is about what that looks like in practice — and why the same things recovery asks of you are exactly what professional life asks of you too.
☕

What You Need to Know

Reliability is its own kind of communicationEvery time you show up on time and follow through on what you said — you are communicating something: I can be counted on. That message, repeated over time, builds the most valuable professional asset you have: trust.
Teams work better when people feel safeResearch from Google found that the single biggest predictor of team performance isn't talent or experience — it's whether team members feel safe to speak up and make mistakes. At The Paddock, the way you treat your teammates shapes how the whole team performs.
Professionalism isn't about perfection — it's about ownershipProfessional people make mistakes. What makes them professional is how they handle it: they own it, fix it, and don't repeat it. The opposite of professional isn't imperfect — it's someone who deflects and blames. Sound familiar? Accountability in recovery and accountability at work are the same muscle.
#1
predictor of team success: feeling safe to speak up — not talent, not experience, not education.
46%
of new hires fail within 18 months due to attitude and communication — not skills or knowledge.

Session 1 · Capstone

Your Personal Brand Statement

What This Is Your Personal Brand Statement is the first document in your Ground Up Plan. It tells the world who you are, what you offer, and why someone would choose to do business with you. You've been building it across four weeks — now you assemble it.
🗂
Ground Up Plan — Deliverable #1
Four weeks of work. One document. Yours. Before you fill in the form below, open Claude.ai and paste in your responses from Weeks 1–4. Ask it to help you assemble them into a Personal Brand Statement of about 100 words. Edit until every word feels true.
📋

What Goes Into Your Personal Brand Statement

Your personal storyWho you are, what you've been through, and what you're building. Honest. Human. Yours. From Week 2.
What you offerThe skills, qualities, and approach you bring to any customer. What someone can count on from you. From Week 4.
Your business idea and why it's yoursWhat you want to build and why this idea connects to who you are. The why matters more than the what. From Week 1.
Your customer service standardsYour three to five commitments to every customer of your business. Brief, specific, and real. From Week 3.
🤖 AI at the Capstone — Assemble and Refine

In Claude.ai, paste in your personal story, your offer, your business idea, and your customer service standards from the four weeks. Then type: "Help me combine these into a single Personal Brand Statement of about 100 words. Keep my voice — warm, honest, and confident. Not corporate."

Review the output. Edit anything that doesn't sound like you. The final version should be something you could say out loud and mean every word of it.
